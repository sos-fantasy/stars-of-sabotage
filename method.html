    How Sabotage scoring works

    Most fantasy formats reward good football. This one rewards the plays that hurt a team: incompletions, sacks, turnovers, missed kicks, and defenses that give up points. Higher totals mean more sabotage.

    How each stat is counted

    Fumbles count for any offensive player, on any play where his team has the ball or he is returning a kick or punt. That includes a quarterback who drops or botches a snap. A lost fumble goes to the last offensive player who lost control of the ball. Fumbles by defensive players never count.

    Field goal distance is the full kick distance: from the spot of the hold to the goalposts. It is the number you hear on the broadcast.

    Blocked field goals and blocked extra points count as misses. A blocked 23-yard try scores the same as a missed 23-yard try.

    Rushing attempts include quarterback kneel-downs, because official NFL statistics record them as carries. Spikes to stop the clock count as incomplete passes for the same reason.

    Points allowed, and why it is not always the final score

    A defense is charged only for points it actually gave up. Points the other team scored against the offense do not count against the defense. We follow the same rule Yahoo uses, since Stars of Sabotage leagues are scored on Yahoo.

    Opponent scores onCounts against the defense?
    Offensive touchdowns and field goalsYes
    Kickoff and punt return touchdownsYes. Special teams belong to the defense unit
    Every extra point and two-point conversionYes, even after a pick six
    Interception return touchdowns (pick sixes)No
    Fumble return touchdownsNo
    Blocked field goal return touchdownsNo
    SafetiesNo
    Defensive two-point returnsNo

    Example

    Arizona at the Giants, 2026 Week 4. The Giants scored 36. Six of those came on a pick six with 11 seconds left, and the game ended without an extra point try. Arizona's defense is charged with 36 minus 6, which is 30. That lands in the 28-34 bucket, so Arizona scores 35 Sabotage points for the week instead of 42.

    The other big platforms draw the line in about the same place. All of them count kick and punt return touchdowns against the defense. ESPN leaves out pick sixes and fumble return touchdowns. CBS Sports leaves out points scored against the offense, naming pick sixes and safeties. Sleeper leaves out touchdowns the defense scores itself, and charges every extra point. The help pages for ESPN and Sleeper do not say how safeties are handled.

    Where the data comes from

    Game statistics come from nflverse, published under the CC BY 4.0 license. We do not use any fantasy platform's data.

    Scores update several times on game days and once each morning. The NFL issues stat corrections from Monday through Wednesday, so every week is marked provisional until 10 AM Eastern on the Thursday after its last game, then final.

    Rule versions

    Every score on this site records the rule version that produced it. If the league changes its rules, past seasons are rescored under a new version, and the old version stays on record.

    The archive goes back to 1999, the first season nflverse has play-by-play for. Every past season is scored under the current rules, including Yahoo's points-allowed rule, so a 2005 score means what it would mean today. Seasons before 2021 had 16 games, so compare per-game numbers across eras.
